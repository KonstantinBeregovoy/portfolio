In my previous post, we explored what <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/interop-2026-why-isnt-just-browser-update-investment-our-beregovoy-eqchf/?trackingId=zoatXoKaRPGwxABr9Q6kJg%3D%3D" target="_blank"><strong>Interop 2026</strong></a> has in store. But a significant question remains behind the scenes: **How do Google, Apple, and Microsoft actually know what we need?**

Many tend to view web standards as a "divine decree" from Cupertino or Mountain View. But the reality is far more interesting: we aren't just consumers - we are **customers.** And we have much more leverage than it might seem at first glance.

### 1. GitHub Diplomacy and the Power of Subscription

Have you noticed how critical features sometimes stay in "discussion" for years? Often, it’s simply a matter of priorities. When you hit **"Watch"** or drop a **"👍"** on a ticket in WICG or Chromium, you’re providing essential social proof.

A comment like *“This issue is blocking our transition to native solutions on a fintech platform with 100k+ users”* carries more weight than a hundred theoretical debates. Right now, I’m personally tracking the progress of **Pointer Events** — it’s my way of voting for interface stability. It might take time, but it’s how we move a ticket from the backlog to an active sprint.

### 2. Surveys are Not Just for Fun

The annual **State of CSS,** <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/posts/konstantin-beregovoy_dont-forget-that-we-use-and-work-according-activity-7360946562316779520-5TIj?utm_source=share&amp;utm_medium=member_desktop&amp;rcm=ACoAACI6lvkBc9sT3fuVCGNxvoqUoIdM-l05iro" target="_blank"><strong>State of HTML</strong></a>**, State of JS**, and others are official compasses for the **Chrome, WebKit, and Mozilla Firefox** teams. They literally look at "Pain Point" charts to form the feature list for the next Interop cycle. Your participation in these surveys is a direct vote in the standards committee. **This actually works.**

### 3. Standards as Fuel for AI

For me, as a Lead Engineer, standardization is also about **AI workflow efficiency**. Tools like **Cursor, Claude, Copilot, and Gemini** perform significantly better on code that follows native standards rather than proprietary "hacks." The fewer "custom bicycles" we build, the faster and more accurately AI tools can generate and verify reliable code.

### 4. Our Path: Reliability Through Testing

Adopting new standards isn't a gamble if your engineering processes are mature. In our team, we follow an iterative approach:

- **Automated tests cover every component in our design system**.

- Changes undergo **visual regression** and **unit testing** immediately. This allows us to adopt Interop features with confidence, knowing that our mission-critical payment flows remain rock-solid.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

Browsers today are an open ecosystem. If you’re missing an API or are tired of a bug, don’t stay silent. Follow the issues, participate in surveys, and implement new standards where they drive real business value.
